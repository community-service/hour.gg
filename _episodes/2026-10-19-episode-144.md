This conversation follows an operations leader using Copilot as a thought partner: to prioritize a 44-project workload, improve team project tracking, and make routine analysis easier without handing over decisions.

## Managing a portfolio

- She mapped projects to her position description and performance goals, then added areas of focus and goal columns to her Excel dashboard. A "maybe someday" list helped separate lower-priority work from year-end commitments.
- Copilot helped surface missing information, unclear next actions, redundant tasks, and vague definitions of done. She used those observations to decide what to defer and how to plan her time.

## Turning records into usable work

- For event expenses, Copilot extracted line items from a 40-page hotel invoice into a spreadsheet. A budget-versus-actual summary revealed the event was 244 percent over budget; the planner traced the underestimate to audiovisual costs not being multiplied by the number of event days.
- For a state-code project, she separated a dense reference sheet into overview, task-list, and reference tabs. Copilot helped build the overview by interviewing the employee one question at a time.

## Coaching, not delegation

- The guest recommends giving Copilot context, inviting it to ask one question at a time, and finishing with "what else should I consider?" The exchange can help less experienced staff clarify scope, risks, owners, approvals, deadlines, and what done means.
- Outputs still need to fit their audience: a simple finance-ready spreadsheet may be more useful than an elaborate redesign. The guest emphasizes her own review and judgment, especially in a compliance-driven workplace, and says she is not trying to automate away the parts of work that support her creative thinking.
